## Disputes

1. **Designer (r2 L68): my ranking "rested on fast swipes"; Win 5.** It rested equally on phone prior art and thin demand, both conceded and unchanged. Win 4.
2. **Designer (r2 L49), return.** No evidence of a daily audio-dodge habit: BlindRun has about 200 installs (https://appgoblin.info/apps/6781393835); leaderboards are cut. Mainstream adults return sometimes, not daily (inference).
3. **Judge (r1 L38): Accessibility award as ceiling, Win 5.** RNIB's toolkit lists evaluating with blind and partially sighted people as a principle (https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/XR_accessibility_insights_and_toolkit_Gaming_sector_report_RNIB_2025.pdf, search summary). S1-R has no such test and no unaided launch. The narrowed wording is honest and credible as design intent, not as validated access; the pool spans both divisions.
4. **Red team (r1 L7): "originality fails twice".** True for Boldest only. No audio-led dodge game was found on any headset, and 2025 winners included familiar genres done hands-first (winners URL above).

## Concessions

- **S1 Win 4 to 3.** Engineer: height cannot be heard through the browser's generic audio. Red team: Quest 3S lacks a headphone jack, and the screen reader I cited is experimental and controller-only. S1's award route closes.
- **S1 now below E3**, not above.
- **S1-R level with N1 and ahead of E3-R** (designer, r2 L68): slow input, no headset rival. Round one had the unrefined idea below both.
