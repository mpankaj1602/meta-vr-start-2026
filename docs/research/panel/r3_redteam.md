### What the designer's conventions leave open

- **Local storage.** The FAQ describes "our large team of globally distributed judges" (`r1_judge.md L60`), so each judge starts with empty storage on a different device. Streaks, lantern strings, growing piles and "tomorrow's question" are never seen. Only what the first run shows counts. Four of the 14 entries target Reason to Come Back on evidence a judge cannot observe.
- **Hand zone.** The convention itself says thigh-level tracking on Quest is unverified (`r2_refined.md L33`). The Engadget quote is about the Glasses; judging will mostly be on Quest 3/3S (`r1_judge.md L60`, inference there). Meta's page still says hands "resting by the thighs ... will not be detected" [HANDS-LIM]. An armrest or tray is assumed; a bus seat has neither.
- **Distance versus the seat test.** Moving objects to 0.6-0.8 m answers the 0.5 m comfort rule [DISPLAY]. It also puts them at or beyond the two-foot (0.61 m) radius. Hands stay inside the radius because selection is indirect, so the test is met on its wording. In passthrough in a tight seat, though, a virtual tray at 0.7 m can sit behind the real seatback (inference). A4-R, P7-R and N3 need a plain-background fallback.
- **Hit-test placement.** It avoids the room scan, which is good for the first minute. It also gives up marks the rubric lists by name: "anchoring content to detected planes, surfaces, and room geometry" [RULES]. P7-R in passthrough "plays the same with passthrough off".
- **Daily seeds under a frozen build.** Each judge gets one seed and the entrant cannot curate after 18 November. The fix is cheap: generate and check every date in the judging window (23 November to 9 December, `r1_judge.md L64`) before submitting.
- **Small targets with a head or hand ray.** Junctions in a 30 cm maze, underlined words on a page and markers on a 25 cm object are small at 0.7-1 m. No entry states a minimum target size for Quest pointing (inference: needs a device check).

## 2. Disputes

1. **Productivity is the best bet because the field is thin** (judge `r1_judge.md L94-100`; engineer `r1_engineer.md L223-227`; designer `r1_designer.md L281`).
   - The claim is inference in every report that makes it. The judge writes "All inference; the project gallery is not yet published"; the designer writes that it is "inference from the announced line-up, not from entry counts" (`r1_designer.md L351`).
   - The prize is 70% of the other tracks ($70k/$35k against $100k/$50k). Productivity must be about 1.4 times easier just to break even (my arithmetic).
   - Hackathon supply is not store supply. Three of the 2025 special-award winners were productivity-shaped camera-plus-AI tools [WINNERS25]. This year's Agentic award ("context-aware assistance") pulls assistant builders toward Productivity (inference).
   - Track relevance is scored twice, under Innovation ("relevance to the chosen track") and Polish ("clearly demonstrate relevance to the chosen track"), and the sponsor may reassign tracks [RULES]. The judge reports that Meta's Productivity exemplar is a file manager (`r1_judge.md L62`).
   - Effect: I allow at most one Win point for field thinness, and only for A4 and A4-R, which fit the track sentence natively. P7-R and N3 get none.
2. **P7-R is evidence-backed and belongs at the top of Productivity** (designer `r2_refined.md L210-211`, Win 6).
   - The studies support a taught strategy for memorising supplied material: virtual loci matched familiar ones and beat uninstructed controls by 10-16% of items [LEGGE] (search summary); a headset beat a desktop by 8.8% [KROKOS] (search summary).
   - P7-R does not teach the strategy for the user's own material. It sets an object-location task and scores it. That is a memory game.
   - Without a benefit claim it has no Productivity purpose; with one it is back to round-one P7.
   - Effect: Win 4, with a relevance penalty.
3. **"Date-seeded puzzles are not a scope trap"** (designer `r2_refined.md L212`).
   - Conceded for build effort (see Concessions).
   - Disputed as a win argument: "daily" proves nothing to a first-run judge, generated puzzles vary in quality, and the build is frozen.
   - Effect: no feasibility penalty for G5-R or N2, and no Reason to Come Back credit either.
4. **Hands can rest in the lap** (designer `r2_refined.md L213`).
   - True for the Glasses on one hands-on report. Unverified for the judging device.
   - Effect: N1 (three minutes of torch) and E3-R (six minutes palm-up) keep a comfort flag.
5. **G5 refined "would rise to about Win 6"** (judge `r1_judge.md L143`).
   - The market analyst found a browser tabletop pinch maze and two other hand-tracked maze titles (`r1_market.md L7`).
   - The designer's own Win is 5 and names familiarity as the main risk.
   - Effect: G5-R stays at 4.
6. **E3 is "the emptiest space found" and Accessibility is "the least targeted" pool** (market `r1_market.md L62, L138`; judge `r1_judge.md L98`).
   - An empty market is not a validated design. The entry is untested with the users it names, launch still needs sight, and it offers two of the four features the award text lists.
   - The pool includes Adapted titles that can add real accessibility modes to shipped products (`r1_judge.md L49`).
   - Effect: E3-R Win 4, not 5.
7. **A7-R keeps A7's strength** (designer `r2_refined.md L88-96`, Win 5; round one Win 6).
   - Judge, engineer and market all rated A7 for tactile evidence handling (`r1_judge.md L26`; `r1_engineer.md L240-241`).
   - A7-R removed it.
   - Effect: A7-R Win 5 with a named fix: restore one physical verb per case (unfold, overlay two papers, hold to the light).
8. **A4 as written deserves Win 7** (judge `r1_judge.md L23`).
   - The same line calls the concept "still vague" and says a judge "cannot feel a habit in one sitting".
   - The 7 includes the thin-field bonus disputed above.
   - Effect: A4 stays at 5; A4-R earns 6 on its specification.
9. **A5 is among the "most buildable and impressive"** (engineer `r1_engineer.md L279`, Win 5).
   - The engineer's praise is for a puzzle "scoped to well-tracked shapes", which is a refinement. As written A5 leads with a fingerspelling tutor.
   - The nearest product dropped eight letters as untrackable and has no ratings (`r1_market.md L22`).
   - The designer withdrew P6-R on the same accuracy evidence (`r2_refined.md L202`).
   - Effect: A5 goes down to Win 3.
10. **P5 Feasibility 3** (engineer `r1_engineer.md L190-195`).
    - The engineer's own table records that the SDK reference says camera frames are not exposed (`r1_engineer.md L34`).
    - The market analyst scores Feasibility 1.
    - Effect: I hold Feasibility 2 until the day-one camera check.

## 5. Per-track ranking

Ranked by Win, then Feasibility, then how few unresolved objections remain.

**Gaming**
1. A3-R Your Move (5 / 4 / 3). A complete solo session with nothing that can fail after the freeze.
2. A7-R Paper Trail (5 / 4 / 3). Finishable; needs one tactile verb restored.
3. A7 Hands-on mystery (6 / 3 / 4). Highest ceiling in the track, but not as written with daily cases.
4. G5-R Lantern (4 / 5 / 2). The fallback if time runs short.
5. N1 Hush (4 / 4 / 3). Only after a one-week prototype proves it is fun.

**Entertainment**
1. A1-R Strand (5 / 4 / 3).
2. A6-R Paper Theatre (5 / 3 / 4).
3. A1 Playable music visualiser (5 / 4 / 3). Same idea as A1-R with the conducting and latency problems still in.
4. A6 Lap pop-up storybook (5 / 3 / 4).
5. E3-R Close Your Eyes (4 / 4 / 3). An award-only play.

**Productivity**
1. A4-R Three Stones (6 / 4 / 2).
2. A4 Daily ritual object (5 / 4 / 2).
3. P7-R Pocket Palace (4 / 4 / 3). Expect a relevance penalty or reassignment.
4. N3 Snip (4 / 3 / 3). The same track risk, plus geometry bugs.
5. A8 Spatial planning board (3 / 3 / 4).

**The entry I would least object to building: A4-R Three Stones.**
- Why: it is the only refined entry whose track fit is the track's own sentence, it has no service, content pipeline or second device to fail, and it is the easiest to finish. Every objection I still hold is about its ceiling, not about a failure.
- This is not an endorsement of the thin-field argument. A4-R earns the place on fit and finishability. If the Productivity field turns out no thinner than the others, it is competing for 70% of the prize.
- Main remaining risk: it is slight. A judge is done in three minutes, cannot feel the habit, and will compare it with a phone list. The stones, the water and the sound have to be good enough to carry that. Passthrough placement and the persistent anchor are also unverified until the entrant has a headset.
- Second choice: A1-R Strand, for the larger prize, if the entrant can judge their own music honestly after a one-week prototype.
