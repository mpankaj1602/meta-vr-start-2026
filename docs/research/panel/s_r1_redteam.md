| S1 | Gaming (casual) | SERIOUS, fixable | The blind phase is an existing phone game with the same four swipes, moved onto worse input (unseen mid-air swipes) and generic audio that reliably separates only left from right | [FEER](https://afb.org/aw/19/11/15145), [WENZEL](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930067961), [HANDS](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-limitations-mitigations/) | One distinct sound per obstacle class; small slow input (thumb microgesture or palm tilt); speaker-first mix; permanent sight-optional toggle; make head turns matter; rename | 3 | 3 | 3 |

Scale: the earlier skeptic's (G4 3/3/3, E3 4/4/2, E3-R and N1 4/4/3). Source keys link on first use. "Inference" is my judgement; "summary" is a search summary, not a full read.

**1. Rules.** Hands-only: passes. Seat test: only with small swipes; G4's defect remains ("rapid or erratic hand movements can challenge the system's tracking accuracy") [HANDS]. New: on a black display nobody sees a hand drift out of camera view (inference). Bus stop: passes. Originality fails twice: the user's own "Subway Surfer type", and FEER (iOS/Android), an audio endless runner with left/right swipes for lanes, down to duck ravens, up to jump [FEER]; BlindRun (iPhone, June 2026) repeats it [BLINDRUN](https://mwm.ai/apps/blindrun/6781393835). "Sonic" plus runner points at SEGA's endless runner [Sonic Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Dash); the [RULES](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules) ban "brand names" (judge reaction is inference).

**2. Perception.** Speed is not the problem: one study measured simple auditory reactions near 284 ms against 331 ms visual [RT](https://coachsci.sdsu.edu/csa/vol191/shelton.htm). Identification is. With non-individualised HRTFs "interaural cues to horizontal location are robust", but front-back and up-down confusions "increased significantly" [WENZEL]; 39 VR listeners rated their own HRTFs easier to localise than generic ones [JENNY](https://games.jmir.org/2020/3/e17576) (summary). Left/right works; jump versus duck cannot rest on elevation. Phone runners use timbre: ravens mean duck, crackling leaves mean jump [FEER]; "Every obstacle has a unique sound", plus a training module [BLINDRUN]. "Spatial" then does one bit of work, which plain stereo also does (inference). The 20-second sighted phase has a rationale, but the supporting study used 60 s, improved azimuth only (1.61 to 1.41 degrees) and found poor transfer between sound types [BERGER](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2018.00021/full).

**3. Why a headset?** It fails "would fall apart on any other platform" [RULES]: phones host it already, with haptics and reliable swipes. Audio-only VR is not new either ([Blind Swordsman, 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-05-13-blind-swordsman.html), summary). A black frame gives nothing to "gaze interactions ... passthrough, spatial anchoring" or "art direction" [RULES]. The headset's one unique asset, head-tracked audio (head rotation resolves front-back ambiguity, [HEAD](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/resolving-front-back-ambiguity-with-head-rotation-the-role-of-lev/), summary), goes unused.

**4. Comfort.** Largely survives: no optic flow, and sound-only self-motion is weaker than visual [VECTION](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/114917). I found no evidence of drowsiness or eye strain in short dark runs. Hardware is the issue: Quest 3S is "the first Meta headset to lack a 3.5mm headphone jack" [Q3S](https://www.uploadvr.com/quest-3s-images-leak-reveal-battery-size-no-headphone-jack/); the nearest phone equivalent is "best played with headphones, wired specifically" because of Bluetooth delay [DRIVE](https://www.pocketgamer.com/blind-drive/review/). It must work on open speakers.

**5. Accessibility.** Double-edged. The gap is real: in 2021 researchers found no accessible commercial VR games for blind players [RACING](https://experts.boisestate.edu/en/publications/racing-in-the-dark-exploring-accessible-virtual-reality-by-develo/). But Meta's screen reader is "experimental" and "requires you to be using Touch controllers" [SR](https://www.meta.com/help/quest/1214695769962939/), so a blind player cannot reach the URL hands-only; speech synthesis is reportedly absent (BRIEF2), so menus need recorded voice; a low-vision tester needed his wife to set up a Quest [EQUAL](https://equalentry.com/virtual-reality-blind-user-testing-meta-quest-2). S1 teaches by sight, then removes the only channel deaf players have (430 million people have disabling hearing loss, [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/deafness-and-hearing-loss)); reaction swipes exclude limited mobility, the award's own example [RULES]. Untested claims often fail: of ten mobile audio games labelled inclusive, three rated "good" [AUDIT](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/169045).

**6. Demo day.** No headphones; office noise masks cues; 20 s cannot teach four sounds and four gestures (all inference). A missed swipe and a misheard cue feel identical; even with touch input a reviewer found audio dodging "a tad unfair" [DRIVE]. The video must "honestly" represent the experience [RULES]: honest footage is black, possibly watched muted (inference).

**7. Hidden scope.** S1 is G4 (a visual runner is still needed for 20 s and the video) plus an audio game: sounds per class with distance cues, recorded menus and tutorial, an unseen-swipe classifier, a tracking-lost cue, and tuning the emulator cannot do (BRIEF2).

**Verdict: SERIOUS, fixable; nothing fatal.** "The lights go out" is a better hook than G4 and removes G4's vection and most art. Rank: above G4 (3.0), below E3 (4.0), E3-R and N1 (4.6); about 26th of 39. Award: Best Accessibility Forward, credible only as S1-R with blind playtesters. Fixed, it reaches 4, no higher before a device prototype.
