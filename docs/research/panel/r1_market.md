## 1. Method and limits

- About 70 web searches and page fetches on 2026-10-06. Reddit was not fetched directly; no claim here rests on Reddit. Community evidence is therefore thinner than press and store-tracker evidence.
- "Not found" means not found in my searches, not proof of absence. Where a novelty claim depends on absence, I say so.
- One fetch failed (Meta community forum, HTTP 403) [38]. The statement that Quest Browser rejects the WebXR `camera-access` feature comes from the search summary of that thread and should be confirmed on a device by the engineer. It decides E1, P5 and P6.
- Win scores are relative, not probabilities. Base rate for context: 2025 had more than 650 entries for 32 awards [9]; 2026 has 20 prizes [2]. If turnout is similar, that is roughly 3% per entry (inference).

## 2. Findings that cut across ideas

1. **Three ideas are already platform features.** E2 (2D to 3D) is shipped by Meta, Google and Apple [40][41][42]. P5 (point and explain) is Meta AI with Vision on Quest 3 and Gemini on Android XR [68][67]. P4 (visit places in Google 3D) is Google Maps immersive view on Android XR, plus FLY and Wander on Quest [67][64][63]. A judge who knows the platform will see these as re-implementations.
2. **Camera-dependent ideas are blocked on the web.** Meta's Passthrough Camera API is for native apps [129]; the WebXR equivalent is reportedly unsupported in Quest Browser [38]. E1, P5 and P6 as written need to see the room.
3. **Last year's winners define what looks derivative.** Mini golf by pinch (Tiny Golf), room re-skin (DreamSpace), room-as-instrument (Inkphony), lap diorama (Pocket Lands), palm-steered ball (Awesome Hand), camera + AI helpers (Language IRL, Chores.gg, Flows.xr), AI robots in WebXR (The Nanauts) [9]. A1, A3, E1 and P5 sit closest to these.
4. **All three 2025 "Best Hand Interactions" winners were one small, novel hand mechanic** (Hand Survivor, Pocket Lands, Awesome Hand) [9]. That matches Meta's "one mechanic" advice.
5. **WebXR no longer has its own award.** In 2025 two IWSDK entries won a dedicated $30k category [9]; the 2026 list has no SDK category [2], so a WebXR entry competes directly with Unity builds. Its real market edge is the link: instant open, join by URL, hand-off to a phone. Meta reported Quest browser use up 21% in 2024 [10].
6. **Sports sims (G1 to G4) are the worst fit to demand and to the brief at once.** The incumbents are controller games with large audiences [19]; hands-only versions have almost none [21][29]; Meta's own guidance says fast motion and hands near the face track poorly and that gestures where the hand "slows or stops" are reliable [4].

## 3. Per-idea notes (what the table does not say)

**G1 Bowling.** Best special award: none obvious. A seated "tabletop bowling" would be read as Tiny Golf with pins. Hands-only bowling exists and nobody noticed it [21].

**G2 Archery.** The draw gesture ends beside the face, where tracking fails [24]. Without haptics there is no string tension. Lowest of the game ideas.

**G3 Rafting.** The only game idea that is naturally seated. But the genre competes on spectacle [27][28], paddling is large fast arm motion, and no hands-only paddling title was found to prove it works.

**G4 Runner.** "Jump, sit or step sideways" is body movement; if remapped to hands it becomes close to Awesome Hand [9]. Existing VR runner has 6 reviews [29].

**G5 Maze.** Best of the user's game ideas on market grounds: puzzle is on-strategy for VR Glasses [5], seated, and no incumbent owns "maze". The risk is that mazes are generic: three hand-tracked maze products already exist, one of them free and one in the browser [26][32]. Award angle: First Five Minutes.

**E1 Anime converter.** DecartXR is the same idea, free, and reviewed as a one-second-lag flat rectangle [36]. It needs a paid GPU service that must stay up through judging. Naming a studio's style conflicts with the IP rule [39].

**E2 3D converter.** The most thoroughly pre-empted idea of the 24. Meta's Instagram app already converts every photo and video; the browser converts web images [40][41]. VR Glasses marketing already promises films "converted to 3D" [6]. Converting commercial films also raises a rights problem.

**E3 Movement through sound.** The most open space found. Nothing audio-first is on the Quest store beyond a sampler [45]; blind players are documented as unserved in VR [48][49]. "Spatial audio" is named in the Entertainment track [2]. Award angle: Accessibility Forward (probably few credible contenders; inference) and Boldest Original Concept. Weakness: as written it is a sensation, not a product, and an eyes-closed app is hard to show in a video.

**P1 Driving.** Demand is real and a free, well-rated incumbent took it in 2026 [51]. Hands-only steering and no pedals make the learning claim weak. "AI adds cars" is a feature without a user request behind it (none found).

**P2 Gardening.** As written this is a plant-care chatbot with pictures; GardenXR already does the camera version [55]. Meta's own WebXR sample is a gardening game [56], so judges have seen web gardening.

**P3 Universe.** Titans of Space PLUS is long-established, loved and already hand-tracked [59]. The gap is interaction: none of the incumbents lets you throw mass around and watch orbits or tidal stretching. "Data from the web" adds risk without demand evidence.

**P4 Vacation visualiser.** FLY is exactly "Google 3D Tiles in a headset", has 1.3K reviews and is on Vision Pro too [64]. Requires a billed Google key and displays Google branding.

**P5 Curious Cat.** Meta ships it [68], Meta open-sourced a sample of it [69], and three entries won with it last year [9]. On the web it probably cannot see the room [38].

**P6 Gym.** Furthest from the brief (standing, public place, machine recognition). Fitness on headsets is crowded at home [71][72][73] and Supernatural is a launch title [6].

**P7 Brain exercise.** The calorie claim would be the first thing a judge challenges; the regulator precedent is direct [77]. Underneath is a sound product shape (short daily seated sessions) with weak incumbents [74]. Award angle: Reason to Come Back.

**A1 Music visualiser.** Named in the track [2] and proven with hands [78], but the most crowded hands genre, with a direct new rival (Blooming Conductor: hands-only, conduct a garden) [57] and Maestro's developer co-presenting Meta's own hands talk [4]. Winning needs audio and art quality above those, from a solo developer.

**A2 AI game master.** Award angle: Agentic Interaction ($25k). Definition of that award was not found; Meta's GDC "agents" content was about developer tooling [4], so the fit is an assumption. wilds.ai launches the same concept with a VR mode during the build window [83]. API cost and uptime are the entrant's.

**A3 Tabletop duel.** Incumbents are weak (18 and 52 reviews) [88][89] and RealCarrom's reviews complain about multiplayer [88]. Join-by-link is a real differentiator that store apps cannot copy. Award angle: Social & Multiplayer. Risk: reads as Tiny Golf's cousin [9]; a judge alone needs a good bot.

**A4 Daily ritual object.** Closest match to the Productivity track wording ("habits tied to a recurring context") [2]. Nearest products are a chatbot desk character [87] and chores gamifiers [94][9]; none is a single-moment ritual. Apple has made persistent room widgets a system feature [17]; no Meta equivalent was found. Award angle: Reason to Come Back. Risk: daily value cannot be felt in one judging session.

**A5 Hand-shape game.** Existing ASL apps are barely used and admit tracking cannot handle eight letters [100][101]. A fingerspelling tutor therefore ships incomplete; the "one-handed puzzle" variant avoids that. Accessibility award fit is debatable: it teaches about accessibility rather than being accessible.

**A6 Pop-up storybook.** No hands-first storybook product found on Quest; prior art is research, an old Magic Leap-era AR piece and a German-language AR reading [104][105][106]. Fits lap, seated, narrow field of view. Award angle: First Five Minutes. Risk is content volume and art quality.

**A7 Hands-on mystery.** Genre is validated by Meta's own slate [113] and by two hand-tracked detective games with good press but small audiences [111][112]. The daily-case format is unoccupied in VR and proven elsewhere [114]. Award angle: Reason to Come Back. Risk: case writing volume; comparison with a Capcom title (not out until 2027).

**A8 Planning board.** Productivity looks like the least contested track: Meta closed its own app [13], announced no productivity titles [5], and the Quest leaders are PC mirrors. Vision Pro shows people do use task apps in a headset [15][16], but those succeed because they sync with a tool people already use; a standalone board has a cold-start problem (inference). Phone hand-off by link helps. Press already doubts multi-window work at 70° [8].

**A9 Interactive comic.** No strong incumbent; existing readers show flat pages [118][119][121]; research supports depth-based panels [120]. Content (original art) is the cost. Meta's streaming partner list includes an anime service [6], which suggests audience overlap (inference).

## 4. Demand signals

**What Meta says**
- Mainstream Adults "purchase VR as a TV replacement first, then discover that it can also play video games", are "mid-core", and "probably play seated without controllers" [3].
- Teens are "currently the most active audience"; discovery happens outside the headset; store visitors "come with a specific title in mind" [3].
- "Space for single player narrative games, but only those that can be made in a very cost-effective way", from "visual novels to adventure games" [3].
- Hands-first suits "casual gamers or non-gamers", "leisure lovers or social explorers", and mechanics built on "surface interactions, physics manipulation, or gesture-driven input" [4].
- Media app time per user rose 10% and browser use 21% in 2024 [10]. In-app purchase revenue grew over 10% in 2025; Horizon+ paid developers nearly $20M [3].
- Retention is the long-standing problem: 2022 internal documents said more than half of headsets were unused six months after purchase [12]; Meta later said Quest 3 retains better [11]. The "Reason to Come Back" award reads as a response to this (inference).

**What the VR Glasses buyer will find on day one**
- Cinema, sports and streaming partners; more than 75 gesture-only games; private virtual screens and a surface keyboard [6][7].
- Announced games are puzzle, card, tabletop, visual-novel and sim titles [5]. Nothing announced for task management, habits, creative tools, reading or accessibility.
- Hands-on press: gestures work with hands resting in the lap; the 70° view forces head turns between windows; sceptical about work use [8].

**What Vision Pro (closest device) shows**
- Survey: 41% of respondents use it daily; uses are Mac display and productivity, media, then some gaming [14].
- Things 3 and Fantastical were rebuilt for it and are repeatedly listed among the most useful apps [15][16].
- Best-games lists are led by tabletop and casual titles (Game Room: chess, solitaire, hearts; LEGO Builder's Journey), with the note that hand tracking "in faster-paced games often feels sluggish" [18].

**What store numbers show (orders of magnitude)**
- Large: ForeVR Bowl ~2,000 reviews [19], Wander 2.2K [63], FLY 1.3K [64], Maestro 847 ratings [78], Road Ready VR 762 [51], Titans of Space 422 [59].
- Small: Wordomi 126 [75], Enhance VR 92 [74], Home Detective 62 [112], Whitewater VR 61 [27], Table Party 52 [89], RealCarrom 18 [88].
- Negligible: Subway Sprinters 6 [29], Silent Classroom 6 [101], Toss-a-palooza 2 [21].
- Reading: hands-only does not create demand by itself; hands-only ports of controller sports have not found users. Categories with big numbers already have owners.

## 5. Open gaps none of the 24 ideas addresses well

1. **Lean-back media companion.** The Entertainment track names "media companion apps" [2] and the device is sold as a television [3][6]. I found no hands-first companion on Quest; the nearest thing surfaced was co-watching in an old Plex VR app [126]. A vendor article estimates over 60% of US sports fans use a second screen during games [127] (vendor figure, unverified). None of the 24 ideas gives the viewer something to do alongside what they came to watch. Evidence of absence is thin.
2. **One headset plus phones, joined by link.** A mainstream adult household likely has one headset (inference). Asymmetric games where others join from a phone browser exist as native VR titles (One vs All, Operation Armstrong, Panoptic II) [92][128][93]; none found that is hands-first, seated and web-native. This is the one social format where WebXR has a structural advantage, and it lets a judge test multiplayer alone with their phone. A3 invites by link but assumes a second headset.
3. **Hands-first visual novel or choice-driven short fiction.** Meta names the genre as under-served [3] and will not have its flagship example until 2027 [113]. The store's known VR visual novel is a 2019-era controller title [122]. A6, A7 and A9 are adjacent, but none is built on gaze-and-pinch choices with voiced or illustrated branching.
4. **Accessibility-first play: blind, low-vision or one-handed.** Documented as unserved [48][49], with a dedicated $25k award [2]. E3 points this way but is not designed for those players as written. Any top idea gains from a fully audio-described, one-hand, no-precision mode.
5. **Lap-scale creative tool with a shareable result.** The Productivity track lists "creative tools" [2]; no creative tool is in the VR Glasses slate [5]. Existing hands tools are pottery, clay and air doodling [123][124][125] with small audiences; a 2025 Judges' Choice went to a hand craft app [9]. None of the 24 ideas makes something the user keeps or sends (A1 is the nearest).

Not a gap, but validated and thinly supplied: a daily-seed spatial puzzle with streak and share. Only a word game and a Wordle port do "daily" on Quest [75][76]. This is the natural direction for G5 and P7.

## 6. Top 5 and bottom 5

**Top 5 (market view)**
1. **A7 Hands-on mystery** (Gaming, Win 6). Genre chosen by Meta and Capcom for this device [113]; existing hand-tracked detectives are well reviewed but small [111][112]; daily cases are unoccupied and proven elsewhere [114].
2. **A4 Daily ritual object** (Productivity, Win 6). Direct match to the track wording [2] and to Meta's retention problem [12]; no close product on Quest; the least crowded track.
3. **A6 Lap pop-up storybook** (Entertainment, Win 6). No hands-first product found; narrative named as under-served [3]; lap scale won last year [9].
4. **E3 Movement through sound** (Entertainment, Win 5). The emptiest space found and a credible Accessibility award target [48][49]; needs to become a concrete product.
5. **A8 Spatial planning board** (Productivity, Win 5). Open on Quest and VR Glasses [5][13], demand shown on Vision Pro [15][16]; held back by field of view [8] and the cold-start problem.

Next: A3 (weak incumbents, link advantage, but derivative of last year's winner) and A2 (clear award target, but a direct rival launches this month [83]).

**Bottom 5**
1. **E2 3D converter** (Win 1). Built into Horizon OS, Android XR and visionOS [40][41][42].
2. **P4 Vacation visualiser** (Win 1). FLY and Wander own it [64][63]; Google does it natively [67]; a wrapper by definition.
3. **P6 Gym spotter** (Win 1). Contradicts seated use; needs camera recognition the web lacks [38]; no demand evidence.
4. **P5 Curious Cat** (Win 2). A Meta AI feature [68], a Meta sample [69] and three 2025 winners [9]; probably blocked on the web [38].
5. **E1 Anime converter** (Win 2). DecartXR exists and is poor [36]; needs camera and a paid GPU service; IP exposure [39].

Close to the bottom: G2 (tracking fails at full draw [24]), P1 (free incumbent with 762 ratings [51]), P2 (LLM lookup; GardenXR exists [55]).
