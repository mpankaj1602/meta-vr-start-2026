Scored as the user describes them. Wording fetched 2026-10-07 from https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/ ; a tool limit stopped further lookups, so comfort points are inference.

**Stage one.** The hard test is "can someone complete the entire experience without ever pairing a controller?" Both pass. But the track is "Games designed for hands-first or eyes and hands", and Quest 3/3S has no eye tracking, so judges see head-first. I would pass both and mark them down in stage two; a strict judge could fail S2, whose loop uses no hand.

**Head input.** Technical names "the features that define hands-first experiences: gaze interactions, hand tracking, passthrough, spatial anchoring, and FoV-aware design". Head tilt is none of these; "gaze interactions" means look-and-pinch targeting. Head pose never drops, so reliability rises while feature use falls: S2 4 (S1 5). Experience ("emphasis on seated, hands-first"): ducking from a heard object needs no teaching, but repeated nods load the neck, and the head that steers is the head that listens. Net 5.

**Audio: where I overstated.** I wrote that generic spatial audio conveys "left/right, not height". Too absolute. The user is right that spectral cues carry up, down and behind, and the browser's filter set includes elevation (s_r1_engineer.md). What holds: with non-individual filters "front-back and up-down confusions ... increased significantly" (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930067961), and a judge may be on open speakers. Jump and duck are viable with a redundant pitch or timbre code; cutting them was unnecessary. "True 3D" alone remains a poor bet. Innovation stays 6: originality up, "hands-first design" down.

**Awards.** Boldest rises from weak to moderate only if dodging is a physical lean or duck: earbuds track head rotation, not position, so a phone cannot do it. Accessibility stays weak: hands-free input helps some players and excludes limited neck mobility.

**"Actual turning if standing"** conflicts with "Design for seated, stationary use. Limit roomscale, no large physical movement". As an option it costs Experience marks; as default, stage one.

**M1, motion-sensitive judge.** Constant forward motion, frequent turns and close corridor walls is the classic discomfort case (inference). That judge removes the headset inside a minute and scores what was seen. Also: looking down a side corridor is a head turn, so looking and steering collide; auto-run removes the thinking a maze needs; a pause gesture is not hands-first.
