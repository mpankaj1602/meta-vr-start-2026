# S1 Sonic Navigator: market analyst, round 1

Method: about 40 searches and fetches, 2026-10-07. AppleVis and a Reddit mirror refused fetches, so no claim rests on forum threads and community voice is thin. "Not found" is not proof of absence.

## Prior art

**The loop already ships on phones.**
- FEER (iOS, Android, 2018): three lanes; swipe sideways to change lane, down to duck, up to jump; played by stereo sound. S1's exact controls. AFB's AccessWorld praised it [1].
- BlindRun (iPhone, June 2026): "endless runner built from the ground up for blind and low-vision players"; a tracker estimates 200 installs [2].
- Blind Drive (PC, mobile, 2021): dodge traffic by ear; 96% of 353 Steam reviews [3]. The quality bar.
- The Vale: 97% of 326 [4]. Soundvoyager (GBA, 2006): eyes-shut car dodging [5]. Blinded (iPhone plus AirPods Pro): head-tracked, eyes-closed sound location [11].

**On headsets the two halves exist apart; none found together.**
- Hand-tracked runners, all visual: LINE BREAKER (free; hands "jump, slide and dodge"; 2 reviews) [6]; Seeker VR (hands or controllers, standing, 4.7★/23) [7]; Subway Sprinters (6 reviews) [8].
- Sound-led VR, none hand-tracked: Echo Tag (Quest, pitch-black multiplayer, 3.5★/34) [9]; Blind (PCVR echolocation, still visual) [10]; Blackout Protocol VR (audiogame for blind players, sideload-only thesis) [13]; research prototypes [14][15].
- Vision Pro, WebXR: nothing found.

## Judgements

1. **Saturation.** Open on Quest, Vision Pro and WebXR. New to the headset, not new.
2. **Platform owner.** No first-party audio-led game. Horizon OS has a screen reader [17]; Beat Saber's modifiers hide cues but stay visual [12].
3. **Mainstream seated adult.** Thin. Audio-first games earn reviews, not volume [3][4]; hands-only runners have almost no users [6][8]. Meta calls its growth audience media-first and seated [21] and says fast hand motion tracks poorly [22]; a twitch swipe game runs against both. "Subway-Surfers-type" keeps the brand comparison that sank G4 (inference).
4. **Blind and low-vision players.** Need documented, numbers absent. Researchers report no commercially available accessible VR games for blind players [15]; RNIB and Abertay's December 2025 toolkit asks for audio-led design tested with blind users [16]; Meta says Cosmonious High's Vision Assistance Mode served 1.53 million descriptions in a month, a "gold standard" [18]. Against: S1 as written is a sighted player's handicap ("initially you see"), not blind-first; Meta's accessibility check advises against "relying on one form of feedback" [19] and hands give no haptics, so the blackout shuts out deaf players; six weeks allows no blind playtesting (inference).
5. **"Would fall apart on any other platform."** Fails as written. FEER and BlindRun run the same loop on a phone with headphones, plus haptic confirmation the headset lacks [1][2]; Blinded puts head-tracked audio on earbuds [11]. A three-lane swipe runner does not need a headset. Boldest Original Concept is not credible.
6. **Name.** "Sonic" on a runner collides with SEGA's Sonic Dash, an endless runner past 500 million downloads [20]; the rules bar third-party IP. Rename (inference).
7. **Showing it.** The blackout is a black frame in the video, as noted for E3.

## Scores and rank

Win 4, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 3. Track: Gaming (Entertainment is lean-back). Award: Best Accessibility Forward Experience, credible only with fully audio menus and onboarding; otherwise Best First Five Minutes.

Rank: about 18th of 39, in the 4.0 band.
- Above **G4** (3): the blackout gives a derivative runner an open headset niche and an award target.
- Above **E3** (4): same Win, but a stated mechanic.
- Below **N1** and **E3-R** (4, Feasibility 4): slow, trackable gestures; neither asks a sighted judge to enjoy losing sight at speed.
- Below every Win-5 entry: phone prior art removes the originality claim.
