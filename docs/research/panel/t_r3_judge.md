# Judge, round 3: S2, M1, S2-R, M1-R

## Disputes

1. **Designer: "Hands now catch" answers hands-first.** Enough for stage one. But "pinch the hand on the chime's side" is two buttons on a beat, not the reach-toward-the-sound catch I proposed, and dodging stays the main verb. Technical 6, level with S1-R.
2. **Designer against market: Boldest "partly" or "still no".** Nearer the market. The test is whether the experience "would fall apart on any other platform"; tilt-dodging ships on earbuds (t_r1_market.md [1][8]), so it degrades, not collapses. The spec declines "couldn't exist anywhere else", the award's first sentence. Fit weak to moderate.
3. **Skeptic: "a judge ducking blind at a desk is the hazard".** Answered: never fully black, desk edge visible, 6 cm moves. S2-R passes the airplane-seat test. The cost is count ("forty leans and bows a night"), and a 3 cm rise sits near the engineer's 2 cm hysteresis (inference).
4. **Engineer: S2 Win 4, "sturdier input".** Three criteria name hands-first, and the engineer's table says "hands idle in play". S2 stays 3.
5. **Market, engineer, designer: M1 Win 3.** G4, a straight runner, is the panel's 3.0; M1 adds forced turns, and the market's own evidence is "I never want to try it again". M1 stays 2.

## Concessions

- S2 Boldest fit: moderate to weak-moderate (market: earbud and Vision Pro tilt games).
- S2 change 4, "nothing urgent from behind": withdrawn; the designer's same-lean rule makes front-back confusion harmless.
- M1 "headset off inside a minute": softened to a risk; Meta's guidance (engineer) supports seated snap turns at 1.4 m/s.
- S2 and M1 numbers unchanged.
