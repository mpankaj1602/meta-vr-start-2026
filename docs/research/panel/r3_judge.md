# Round 3: Competition Judge — disputes, concessions, final scores

Read in full: r1_market.md, r1_engineer.md, r1_redteam.md, r1_designer.md, r2_refined.md. Refined entries are scored from r2_refined.md only. Rubric and award wording are from the official pages cited in my round-one report.

## 1. Disputes

**D1. Designer: indirect pointing as the house style for every entry.**
- Claim: r2 conventions, "'Point and pinch' means the system pointer plus a pinch ... Selection is indirect, so nothing has to be within reach."
- Counter: on the Quest 3/3S that judges will mostly use, that is a ray and a click. The Technical criterion names "hand tracking" as a feature to be used strategically, and Meta's hands guidance says to "prioritize direct manipulation of physical objects over 2D menu interactions". Six refined entries are pointer-only or nearly so (P7-R, G5-R, N2, A7-R, P5-R, A4-R). In the other eight the hands do something a pointer cannot: N3 (draw a cut, pull open), N1 (torch in the hand), E3-R (catch and pour in the palm), A1-R (pull a strand), A2-R (dice), A6-R (tabs and wheels), A3-R and P3-R (pull and release).
- Effect: I cap Technical at 5/10 for the pointer-only six. That is the main reason N3, N1 and E3-R outrank P7-R, G5-R and N2.

**D2. Designer: P7-R is the pick and belongs in Productivity.**
- Claim: "Current pick: P7-R"; track "Productivity (may be reassigned to Gaming)"; "P7-R gives a result inside the session".
- Counter: stage one passes. The track is the problem. As specified, "the daily ten is drawn by date from a fixed pool", which is a memory game; only the "My Day" deck touches the track sentence. I would not bother to reassign it, but "relevance to the chosen track" is scored in Innovation and again in Polish ("clearly demonstrate relevance to the chosen track"), so it loses marks twice. The designer's own source lists a prior memory-palace app (Librarium), and the look is stock CC0 kits.
- Effect: P7-R Win 5 in Productivity (designer 6). If a panel moved it to Gaming I would score it 3: a mid puzzle in the strongest field.

**D3. Designer: N3 is a Win 5 with reassignment as its main risk.**
- Claim: "it may read as a toy and be reassigned out of Productivity"; designer Win 5.
- Counter: there is nowhere better to move it. It is not lean-back media and not a game, and "creative tools" is verbatim track text. It is the only entry that uses passthrough the way the Judging page describes ("walls host content"), with a persistent anchor (named under Innovation), one mechanic, and a payoff in 60-90 seconds. Symmetry hiding sloppy lines is exactly the design answer to tracking noise. It has three prize paths: the Productivity track, Best First Five Minutes, and Judge's Choice ("standout entries that don't fit neatly into a single track"); a hand craft app took a Judges' Choice in 2025. My one novelty search found paper-folding and drawing apps but no paper-cutting app (thin evidence; sources below).
- Effect: N3 Win 7, my highest. Toy perception costs it one or two points of 40 on relevance, which I have priced in.

**D4. Designer: A7-R improves A7.**
- Claim: A7-R cuts "a pin board" and (round one) "3D evidence props"; mechanic is "notice, lift, slot" on word chips; target Best First Five Minutes.
- Counter: the cuts removed what I scored. Handling evidence was the hands-first, spatial part; what remains is reading papers and clicking underlined words, which plays identically on a tablet (it is the fill-the-statement mechanic of a well-known flat-screen detective game; my recollection, not looked up). The First Five Minutes definition says "without a wall of text"; a text game is an awkward claimant.
- Effect: A7-R Win 4 (designer 5), below A7 as written at 5.

**D5. Designer: four entries target Best Reason to Come Back with a date seed and an archive.**
- Claim: conventions, "Every daily entry includes a 'this week' archive so a judge sees progression in one sitting"; A4-R, P7-R, G5-R and N2 all name this award.
- Counter: the definition asks for "thoughtful progression and reward design: something to work toward, visible growth between sessions". A seeded puzzle plus a streak is the minimum form of that, and the pool is cross-division, so it includes shipped titles with real progression systems. A4-R (the pile) and G5-R (the lantern string) at least show growth; N2 and P7-R show a streak.
- Effect: I give none of the four meaningful odds on that award. Their Win scores rest on track placement only.

**D6. Skeptic: A4 is a 5 because a phone is faster and daily cannot be judged.**
- Claim: "A judge cannot experience 'daily' in one sitting ... planes need room setup ... text entry for tasks is unsolved"; designer adds "A phone list is faster."
- Counter: half conceded (see Concessions). But A4-R answers each technical point with features the engineer confirmed: hit test without a room scan, the system keyboard, one auto-persisted anchor, plus a seven-day preview. "Faster on a phone" is the wrong test: the track rewards apps "designed around specific daily moments" and Experience Design scores "habit-forming purpose", not throughput.
- Effect: A4-R Win 6. It is the most on-message Productivity entry; its ceiling is limited by a three-minute session.

**D7. Skeptic and designer: A2-R cannot satisfy the take-it-away test and the Agentic definition together.**
- Claim: skeptic, "LLM only as optional narration"; designer, "I am not confident this satisfies both tests at once."
- Counter: the rules impose both, and an offline deck with a model directing through tools is the right architecture for both. The real failure is visibility. With voice and free text cut, the judge picks one of three buttons, and cannot tell a model from a script in seven minutes. "AI is a core part of the experience" has to be seen to be scored.
- Effect: A2-R Win 4, award fit moderate. Fix (suggestion, not scored): show the director's reasoning and let one beat per room take a free choice.

**D8. Designer: A3-R targets Best Social & Multiplayer.**
- Claim: "play against a friend's phone by trading four-character shot codes".
- Counter: the definition is "Experiences designed for shared presence". Exchanging codes is correspondence play with no presence. G5-R's optional phone navigator (a friend sees the walls and guides by voice) is closer to "asymmetric multiplayer" and "cooperative play" than anything in A3-R, though it is marked "cut first if late".
- Effect: A3-R award "none", Win 4.

**D9. Designer: P3-R as a Gaming puzzle ending at the black hole.**
- Claim: "ships 24 levels ... ending at a black hole that stretches your own hand"; target Best First Five Minutes.
- Counter: two problems. Moving from Entertainment to Gaming puts last year's winning verb into the most crowded track (the designer says so: "shares its verb with last year's Tiny Golf"). And the one image a judge would remember sits at level 24; at "1-2 minutes" a level, nobody judging for five to ten minutes sees it. The award asks for "a payoff before the user takes the headset off".
- Effect: P3-R Win 4 (designer 5). Putting the black hole in level three would be worth a point.

**D10. Engineer: A5 is a top-five build at Win 5 with an Accessibility angle; G4 is a 4.**
- Claim: engineer ranks A5 third "most buildable and impressive" and lists "Accessibility Forward" for it; G4 Win 4.
- Counter: the award is for accessible design ("voice alternatives ... eyes-only navigation ... high-contrast modes ... inclusive by default"), not for teaching sign language. The market analyst found an existing tutor that dropped eight letters as untrackable, and the skeptic cites roughly 10 degrees of joint-angle error. For G4, "Subway-Surfers-type" caps originality by name and fast swipes are Meta's stated worst case.
- Effect: A5 Win 3, G4 Win 3.

Smaller point: the market analyst ("Definition of that award was not found") and the designer's round-one Appendix D both say the award definitions are unpublished. They are on the rules and special-awards pages; the designer corrected this in round two.

No Gaming entry reaches 6. It is the most crowded track, and most of the refined games were made safer by making them pointer-driven.

**Overall recommendation: N3 Snip, entered in Productivity.**

- **Main reason:** it has the highest expected score across the four criteria (about 28/40 by my estimate, against 26 for A4-R, A6-R and N1). It is one mechanic the hands perform directly, pays off inside 90 seconds, and uses passthrough and a persistent anchor in the rubric's own terms. It also has three routes to a prize where most entries have one.
- **Main risk:** a panel reads it as a toy rather than a "creative tool", marking down track relevance, and it has only a weak reason to return.
- **What would reduce the risk (suggestion, not scored):** tie it to one recurring moment and give it a keepable result, such as an export to print or send, with the wall gallery growing across sessions.
- **Fallback:** A4-R if the entrant wants the lowest-variance, most on-message Productivity build.
