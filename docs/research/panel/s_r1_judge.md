S1 as written: 20/40. Feasibility and Difficulty defer to the engineer.

**Stage one.** Pass. Swipes cover every action, play is seated, "casual" is named in Gaming. Condition: restart and menus must work by hand in the dark.

**Criteria**
- Innovation 6 (G4 4, E3 7). The runner is derivative; lights-out is what a judge remembers. The listed capabilities ("spatial depth, hands-first design, gaze, passthrough mode, scene understanding, persistent anchors") go mostly unused; spatial audio is not among them.
- Experience 5 (G4 6). See-then-hear is good wordless teaching, but: 20 s cannot teach four sound-to-gesture pairs; the browser's generic spatial audio conveys left/right, not height, so jump versus duck cannot be heard spatially, least of all on built-in speakers; nothing confirms a swipe registered, so tracking drops feel like unfair deaths; every retry replays 20 easy seconds.
- Technical 5. Of "gaze interactions, hand tracking, passthrough, spatial anchoring, FoV-aware design" it shows hand tracking only, as a four-way D-pad built on the motion Meta warns about ("rapid or erratic hand movements"). FoV-awareness is met vacuously.
- Polish 4 (E3 5). Art direction is scoreable for 20 seconds; sound design carries everything. An honest video is about 2.5 minutes of black, heard through laptop speakers that flatten the cue.

**Awards against the wording**
- Accessibility Forward: partial. Sight-optional after 20 s, but teaching is visual, there are no "voice alternatives", fast swipes work against "limited mobility", no "high-contrast modes", and deaf players are shut out, so not "inclusive by default". Blindness as difficulty is a blindfold, not access. Weaker than E3-R.
- Boldest: weak. "Would fall apart on any other platform" does not hold: FEER, an audio endless runner (jump, slide) for blind and sighted players, has been on phones since 2019 (https://afb.org/aw/19/11/15145 , https://apps.apple.com/app/id1422421760). In the dark the headset is head-tracked headphones plus a hand sensor, display off.
- First Five Minutes: moderate ("I dodged blind" at ~30 s), undermined by swipe reliability.

**Rank.** My list, now 39: 24th, last of the Win 4 band (lowest raw score in it), above the four Win 3s. Panel table: level with the 4.0 group (ranks 16-21).
- Above G4 (3): same 20/40, plus a memorable hook and an award route.
- Level with E3 on Win; behind on raw (20 v 22) and track crowding.
- Below E3-R (5): better award fit, tracker-safe palm input.
- Below N1 (5): same hear-the-threat idea, slow aiming, visuals kept.

**Track.** Gaming. Entertainment names "spatial audio" and is thinner, but is "lean-back"; a twitch score-chaser loses relevance marks under Innovation and Polish and invites reassignment. 20/40 places in neither; the prize route is a cross-track special award.

**Changes that would raise it (not scored)**
1. Input by position, not velocity: one supported hand held left/centre/right, a tone reporting where it is, a tracking-lost sound.
2. Three lanes by pan; drop height or encode it by rhythm. Mix for built-in speakers.
3. Blind-first from second zero: spoken onboarding, audible menus, a sound for every recognised input; tempo settings, head-lean steering, high-contrast mode, visual cues for deaf players.
4. Earn the dark: vision fades with success and pulses back after a hit; this also feeds the video.
5. Start in passthrough; dim the real room to black.
6. Obstacles on a beat, hiding tracking delay.
7. Video: "wear headphones" card, labelled spectator overlay.
8. New fiction; drop the Subway Surfers framing.

Estimate: 25-26/40, Win 5, level with N1 and E3-R; the ceiling is the Accessibility award.
