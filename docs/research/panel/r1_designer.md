## Refined entries in full

### P7-R — Pocket Palace
- **Pitch:** a five-minute daily memory workout: place ten things around a lap-sized house, then prove you remember them, now and tomorrow.
- **Track / award:** Productivity (commute habit; stage-one fit is the main risk, fallback Gaming-puzzle). Best Reason to Come Back.
- **Mechanic:** place, then recall. This is the method of loci (the "memory palace" technique).
- **Hands:** look at a lit spot (bath, stove, bed), pinch with the hand in the lap; the item drops in with a sound gag. Recall: pinch an item on the tray, pinch its spot. Rounded targets about 8 cm apart.
- **Seat / stop:** the house is about 35 cm wide (about 39° at 50 cm) and sits below eye line. A round is 4 minutes; progress is saved after every item.
- **Device use:** spatial depth is the function, not decoration; anchored to a tray table or the lap; passthrough keeps a commuter aware.
- **Return:** it opens with "what was in the bath yesterday?"; streak; the house gains rooms.
- **Cut:** typed lists, voice, multiplayer, first-person walking. Keep a "My Day" deck picked from an icon shelf.
- **Assets:** Kenney and Quaternius kits (CC0), Fluent Emoji (MIT). **Online:** static site only.
- **Evidence kept honest:** virtual loci work as well as familiar ones (Legge 2012); head-tracked displays helped recall (Krokos 2019); testing beats re-reading (Roediger and Karpicke 2006). No "sharper brain" or calorie claims.
- **Scores:** Win 6, Feasibility 4, Difficulty 3.

### N1 — Hush (merges E3's "movement through sound" with a game)
- **Pitch:** small creatures creep toward the lamp in your lap, but only while you are not looking; you find them by ear.
- **Track / award:** Gaming (casual). Boldest Original Concept.
- **Mechanic:** look to freeze, pinch to catch. Head direction steers a 20° lamp beam; everything outside it is dark and audible.
- **Hands:** one pinch, hand resting in the lap. No reaching, no hover needed.
- **Seat / stop:** creatures arrive within ±35° of centre, so head turns stay small. Rounds last 3 minutes; pause is "close the lamp".
- **Device use:** turns the narrow view into the rule of the game ("FoV-aware design" appears twice in the rubric); spatial audio carries the information; works the same with head pointing on Quest and gaze on Glasses.
- **Return:** a daily pattern shared by everyone, plus three difficulty lamps.
- **Cut:** story, levels, multiple creature types beyond three, passthrough placement.
- **Assets:** procedural blobs; footsteps self-recorded or CC0. **Online:** static site.
- **Risks:** fun is unproven until prototyped; needs a visual edge-glow option for players who cannot localise sound.
- **Scores:** Win 6, Feasibility 4, Difficulty 3.

### A7-R — Paper Trail
- **Pitch:** a detective game made entirely of paper: read the dossier in your lap, lift the details that matter, complete the statement.
- **Track / award:** Gaming (narrative; Meta names narrative as under-served). Best Reason to Come Back.
- **Mechanic:** notice, lift, slot. Each document has up to seven large hotspots (a stamp, a date line, a signature). Lifted details become word chips; chips fill blanks in the solution.
- **Hands:** look at a hotspot and pinch; look at a blank and pinch. Pinch-drag slides documents. All indirect, hands in lap.
- **Seat / stop:** dossier about 40 cm wide; a daily case is three documents and 3-5 minutes; the flagship case is 10.
- **Device use:** 37 pixels per degree makes reading pleasant; gaze as "noticing" fits the fiction; dossier anchored to a real table.
- **Return:** a daily docket; ship one flagship plus twelve dailies.
- **Cut:** 3D evidence props, voice, branching dialogue, characters' faces (silhouettes only).
- **Assets:** documents typeset by the entrant with open-licence fonts; paper foley self-recorded. **Online:** static site.
- **Risk:** writing quality is the whole product.
- **Scores:** Win 6, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 3.

### E3-R — Close Your Eyes (absorbs A1)
- **Pitch:** a six-minute wind-down you play by hand with your eyes shut: catch falling notes in your palm and pour them into a bowl of sound.
- **Track / award:** Entertainment (spatial audio, lean-back). Best Accessibility Forward Experience; second choice Boldest Original Concept.
- **Mechanic:** hold a sound in an open palm, tilt to pour. The sound's position tracks the hand, so hearing replaces haptics.
- **Hands:** either hand, palm up in the lap (a restful pose that faces the cameras). No pinch precision, no specific hand.
- **Seat / stop:** nothing leaves the lap; one piece is 6 minutes; both palms down pauses.
- **Device use:** hand tracking and head-tracked spatial audio as the entire interface; optional visuals show each note as a bead of light for deaf and hard-of-hearing players and for the demo video.
- **Return:** a different generative piece each evening; a wind-down slot.
- **Cut:** conducting, instruments, recording, any menu beyond start.
- **Assets:** synthesised tones (Web Audio); spoken onboarding recorded by the entrant. **Online:** static site.
- **Honest limits:** not tested with blind players in six weeks; launching a URL still needs sight or the experimental screen reader.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 3.

### G5-R — Lantern
- **Pitch:** a daily maze the size of a dinner plate: you see only what your lantern lights, with three lifts and five arrows to get out.
- **Track / award:** Gaming (puzzle). Best Reason to Come Back.
- **Mechanic:** move by look-and-pinch along lit corridors; spend a lift to rise above the walls for three seconds; drop or remove an arrow at a junction. This keeps both of the user's original abilities.
- **Hands:** pinch to step. Pinch-hold and raise the hand a few centimetres to lift (small indirect motion, amplified). Hands stay in the lap.
- **Seat / stop:** maze about 30 cm, below eye line; a maze takes 3-6 minutes; position is saved every step.
- **Device use:** depth makes "rising above" readable; maze anchored to a tray table; lantern glow suits OLED blacks.
- **Return:** one seeded maze a day for everyone; result (steps, lifts used) shared as a link.
- **Cut:** first-person view, enemies, multi-floor mazes.
- **Assets:** fully procedural. **Online:** static site.
- **Weakness:** mazes are familiar; innovation score will be middling.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 5, Difficulty 2.

### P3-R — Perigee
- **Pitch:** sling a probe around planets on your lap using real gravity, one launch at a time.
- **Track / award:** Gaming (puzzle). Best First Five Minutes.
- **Mechanic:** pinch, pull back, release. A dotted preview shows the first seconds of flight; planets bend the path; reach the beacon in as few launches as par.
- **Hands:** the pull is a few centimetres in the lap, amplified (the same indirect pattern Apple shows for What the Golf).
- **Seat / stop:** system about 40 cm across; a puzzle takes 1-2 minutes; ten in the first session.
- **Device use:** true black space on micro-OLED; a visible dip grid shows "what gravitational acceleration looks like"; one black-hole level delivers the user's spaghettification moment as a fail state.
- **Return:** daily system with par; link to share.
- **Cut:** live web data, falling-speed calculator, free-roam universe.
- **Assets:** procedural spheres and trails; Newtonian integrator, no physics engine. **Online:** static site.
- **Weakness:** gravity-golf exists on flat screens; same verb as last year's Tiny Golf.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 4, Difficulty 3.

### P5-R — Curio
- **Pitch:** every day a real museum object lands in your lap unlabelled; turn it, question it, and work out what it is.
- **Track / award:** Gaming (casual). Best Reason to Come Back.
- **Mechanic:** inspect, then deduce. Five to seven hotspots on the object each reveal a verified clue from the cat; then choose what it is, how old, how it was made or formed.
- **Hands:** pinch-drag anywhere to rotate (indirect); look at a hotspot and pinch.
- **Seat / stop:** one object, about 25 cm, below centre; 3-4 minutes.
- **Device use:** scanned detail at 37 pixels per degree; depth and rotation are how clues are found.
- **Return:** one object a day, streak, a shelf of solved objects.
- **Cut:** camera, touching real things, any live model. Clue text is written and checked against the museum record, so it works offline.
- **Assets:** Smithsonian Open Access 3D scans (over 2,000, CC0), decimated and compressed; credited plainly, no museum branding. **Online:** static site.
- **Weakness:** risks reading as a model viewer; asset pipeline is real work.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 3.

### A2-R — The Room Rolls
- **Pitch:** a seven-minute solo adventure where the game master builds the room in front of you and you settle everything with dice.
- **Track / award:** Gaming. Best Agentic Interaction.
- **Mechanic:** choose, then roll. The model acts through tools (place tile, spawn prop, move token, call for a roll, end scene), so its decisions appear as physical changes, not chat.
- **Hands:** look-and-pinch one of three choices; grab the dice and tip them out with a small wrist turn in the lap.
- **Seat / stop:** diorama about 35 cm; one room per session; the scene state is saved after every beat.
- **Device use:** hand-thrown physics dice; the dice roll covers model latency.
- **Return:** your character sheet persists; a new room each day.
- **Cut:** voice input and spoken narration (the Web Speech API is reported unsupported in Quest Browser), open-ended text, combat systems.
- **Assets:** CC0 dungeon kits. **Online:** a small proxy with a paid model key, rate limits, and a scripted fallback room if the call fails.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 4.

### A3-R — Your Move (absorbs G1)
- **Pitch:** a flick-duel you play by post: take one shot, send the link, get on with your day.
- **Track / award:** Gaming. Best Social & Multiplayer.
- **Mechanic:** pinch, pull back, release a puck on a round board; nearest the centre scores.
- **Hands:** small indirect pull in the lap; aim is set at the pinch.
- **Seat / stop:** board about 35 cm; a turn takes 30 seconds, which is the shortest session in this list.
- **Device use:** board anchored to a real table; replay of the opponent's shot plays out in front of you.
- **Return:** it is your turn. That is the strongest return hook there is.
- **Web advantage:** the opponent can answer from a phone browser, so a judge with one headset can still play a real match.
- **Cut:** real-time play, lobbies, voice, bowling and other variants.
- **Assets:** procedural board and pucks. **Online:** a tiny key-value store for match state, plus a bot.
- **Weakness:** RealCarrom, Table Party and Tiny Golf already own this verb.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 4.

### A6-R — Paper Theatre
- **Pitch:** a six-scene papercut tale in a box on your lap, each scene worked by one paper mechanism.
- **Track / award:** Entertainment (immersive storytelling). Best First Five Minutes.
- **Mechanic:** work the paper: a pull tab, a wheel, a flap, a slider, a string, a lantern. One per scene.
- **Hands:** look at the tab and pinch-drag a few centimetres; indirect, hands in lap.
- **Seat / stop:** theatre about 35 cm wide with six depth layers; the whole tale is 6-8 minutes; resumes at the scene.
- **Device use:** layered silhouettes give real parallax when the head moves; black paper against warm backlight suits OLED.
- **Return:** weak. A second tale would be needed; say so in the submission.
- **Cut:** character animation beyond hinges and slides, voice acting (captions plus the entrant's own narration), page turning.
- **Assets:** silhouettes drawn as vector shapes by the entrant; a public-domain fable or an original text.
- **Online:** static site.
- **Scores:** Win 5, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 4.

### A4-R — Three Stones (absorbs A8)
- **Pitch:** a morning and evening ritual: pick the three things that matter today, and give them back tonight.
- **Track / award:** Productivity. Best Reason to Come Back.
- **Mechanic:** pinch a token from an icon shelf and set it in one of three hollows; in the evening drop the finished ones in a jar.
- **Hands:** look-and-pinch, pinch-drag; hands in lap.
- **Seat / stop:** tray about 30 cm; 60-90 seconds per visit.
- **Device use:** anchored to the same desk spot each day; the jar filling over weeks is the record.
- **Return:** morning and wind-down are named in the track description.
- **Cut:** voice capture, typing, calendars, multi-panel boards; the day's three can be opened on a phone by link.
- **Assets:** procedural stones, open-licence icons. **Online:** static site, local storage.
- **Honest weakness:** a phone does this in ten seconds; the case rests on ritual, not utility.
- **Scores:** Win 4, Feasibility 4, Difficulty 2.

### P6-R — Loosen (absorbs A5)
- **Pitch:** a two-minute reset for the only parts of you this device can measure: hands and neck.
- **Track / award:** Productivity (break habit). Best Reason to Come Back.
- **Mechanic:** follow ghost hands through five finger positions and four slow head turns; each held pose fills a ring.
- **Hands:** static shapes held in view at chest height for a few seconds each, either hand, generous tolerance.
- **Seat / stop:** nothing leaves a two-foot radius; 2 minutes; resumes mid-set.
- **Device use:** hand tracking and head tracking are the sensors; no pinch needed.
- **Return:** a break between tasks, tracked as a streak.
- **Cut:** gym machines, body tracking, scenes, any treatment claim.
- **Evidence kept honest:** short breaks show small gains in vigour and fatigue, none proven for performance; gliding exercises have limited evidence. Framed as a movement break with a disclaimer.
- **Assets:** procedural. **Online:** static site.
- **Risk:** closed-fist poses self-occlude; needs on-device tuning.
- **Scores:** Win 4, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 3.

### A9-R — Slumberland, Nightly
- **Pitch:** one page of a 1905 dream comic each night, unfolded into depth, ending as the boy wakes up.
- **Track / award:** Entertainment. Best First Five Minutes.
- **Mechanic:** pinch to open the next panel; panels unfold around the centre of view in layers.
- **Hands:** a single pinch, hand in lap.
- **Seat / stop:** one page is 2-3 minutes; the page resumes at the panel.
- **Device use:** crisp line art at 37 pixels per degree; head movement gives parallax between cut layers.
- **Return:** a wind-down page a night; every strip ends with waking, which suits bedtime.
- **Cut:** animation, narration, anything after 1906.
- **Assets:** Winsor McCay's Little Nemo, first run, public domain; layers cut and in-painted by hand for about eight pages.
- **Risks:** judges may read it as repackaging; later strips include a racist caricature (my recollection, verify), so page selection must be strict.
- **Online:** static site.
- **Scores:** Win 4, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 3.

### G2-R — Stillbow
- **Pitch:** slow archery where your eyes aim and two fingers draw.
- **Track / award:** Gaming. None realistic.
- **Mechanic:** look at the target, pinch to fix the aim, pull back to set power against wind, release.
- **Hands:** one hand, a pull of a few centimetres in the lap; no bow arm, no raised elbows.
- **Seat / stop:** nothing leaves the lap; twelve arrows take 3 minutes; the round resumes at the arrow.
- **Device use:** the clearest showcase of gaze aiming on Glasses, but on Quest it is head aiming, which is what judges will mostly see.
- **Return:** a daily wind and target layout; score to beat.
- **Cut:** bow model in hand, moving targets, two-hand draw.
- **Assets:** procedural range and targets; self-recorded string and thud. **Online:** static site.
- **Scores:** Win 4, Feasibility 4, Difficulty 2.

### E2-R — Open Frame (carries what is salvageable from E1)
- **Pitch:** one public-domain painting a day, opened into layers you can pull apart, with a two-minute soundscape.
- **Track / award:** Entertainment. None realistic.
- **Mechanic:** pinch-drag to separate depth layers; release and they settle.
- **Hands:** look at the painting, pinch and pull a few centimetres toward you; hands in lap.
- **Seat / stop:** the frame sits about a metre away inside the central 40°; one painting is 2-3 minutes.
- **Device use:** head movement gives parallax between layers; fine brushwork at 37 pixels per degree.
- **Return:** a new painting each day.
- **Why this form:** depth is authored offline per painting, so there is no live conversion and no third-party film.
- **Cut:** user uploads, video, any on-device depth model.
- **Assets:** CC0 museum images, layered and in-painted by hand for about twelve works. **Online:** static site.
- **Weakness:** system-level 2D-to-3D features will make this feel ordinary.
- **Scores:** Win 4, Feasibility 4, Difficulty 3.

### P1-R — Hazard Eyes
- **Pitch:** a five-minute hazard-spotting drill from the driver's seat.
- **Track / award:** Productivity (learning; stage-one fit doubtful). None realistic.
- **Mechanic:** look at the developing hazard and pinch the moment you see it; earlier scores higher.
- **Hands:** a single pinch, hand in lap. The aim direction at the pinch shows whether you were looking at the hazard.
- **Seat / stop:** no wheel, no pedals, no raised arms; the scene plays in a windscreen-sized window to limit motion discomfort; a clip is 40 seconds, a drill is six clips.
- **Device use:** gaze-and-pinch is the task itself; on Quest it degrades to head pointing.
- **Return:** a daily drill with a reaction-time trend.
- **Cut:** steering, AI-generated cars, licence-test claims.
- **Evidence:** training improves hazard anticipation; links to crash rates are small and mixed.
- **Assets:** Kenney city and car kits (CC0); twelve scripted street scenes, which is heavy content for one person. **Online:** static site.
- **Scores:** Win 4, Feasibility 3, Difficulty 4.

## Top 5 and my pick

1. **P7-R Pocket Palace** — Win 6, Feasibility 4.
2. **N1 Hush** — Win 6, Feasibility 4.
3. **A7-R Paper Trail** — Win 6, Feasibility 3.
4. **E3-R Close Your Eyes** — Win 5, Feasibility 3; the best special-award-only play.
5. **G5-R Lantern** — Win 5, Feasibility 5; the safest thing to finish.

**I would build P7-R, Pocket Palace, targeting Best Reason to Come Back.** Reasons:

- **The track is thin.** No productivity, education or creative titles were announced for the Glasses, and Workrooms is gone. A polished, on-brief habit app faces a weaker field than any game does *(inference: I have no entry counts)*.
- **Space is the function.** Most ideas here use depth as dressing. A memory palace cannot exist without it, which answers "why is this in a headset?" directly.
- **The first five minutes contain a real result.** A newcomer recalls ten items in order and is surprised by it. That moment is earned by a documented technique, not by spectacle.
- **The return hook is built into the method.** Tomorrow's first question is about today's house. No notifications or content treadmill are needed.
- **It suits this developer.** Look-and-pinch only, hands in the lap, static hosting, CC0 kits, no model key, no netcode. Head-pointing on Quest works identically.

What would change my mind: if the panel judges that a memory trainer fails the Productivity theme check, I would move to N1 (if a one-week prototype is fun) or G5-R (if it is not). The "My Day" deck is what keeps P7-R inside the track, so it should not be cut.

## Appendix A — as-written ideas: what the first five minutes feel like

- **G1 Bowling.** You swing an empty hand; with no weight or haptics the release point is a guess, and a fast swing is where tracking drops, so balls leave late or sideways. Three frames are fun, then the arm tires. It cannot be done in an airplane seat. Award: none.
- **G2 Archery.** Bow hand up, draw hand at the cheek: the hands occlude each other close to the face, and both arms stay raised. The first shots feel great; by minute four the shoulders ache. Award: none.
- **G3 Rafting.** Continuous two-arm strokes against no resistance while water rushes past. Seeing motion while sitting still (vection) is the standard trigger for discomfort. Award: none.
- **G4 Runner.** Swipes steer a three-lane runner. It is fun for a minute, then mis-read swipes end runs, because a reaction game punishes any tracking delay. The look must also avoid the named game's trade dress. Award: none.
- **G5 Maze.** As written this reads as a first-person maze: corridors fill a narrow view and "jumping above" is a vertical camera move. Dropping and lifting arrows is the satisfying part; wandering is slow. Award: Best Reason to Come Back, weakly.
- **E1 Anime converter.** If it worked, a minute of delight and then nothing to do. It needs live camera frames plus real-time neural restyling in a browser; camera frames are reported unavailable to WebXR in Quest Browser. "Ghibli" is another studio's name. Award: none.
- **E2 3D converter.** You pick a clip and wait. The content belongs to someone else, per-frame depth is not real-time in a browser, and system features already do this for photos. Award: none.
- **E3 Sound movement.** Sounds circle your head with eyes closed: pleasant and unlike anything else here. As written the hands have nothing to do and there is no arc, so by minute three it is a screensaver for the ears. Award: Boldest Original Concept or Accessibility Forward.
- **P1 Driving.** Hands hold an invisible wheel at chest height, with no pedals. The novelty lasts two minutes; then arms ache and it is clear nothing will transfer to a real car. Award: none.
- **P2 Gardening.** Choose a crop, read a care card, look at growth pictures. It is a reference page floating in space, and wrong advice has a real cost. Award: none.
- **P3 Universe.** Float past planets, drop a ball on the Moon and on Jupiter, fall into a black hole. One strong moment, but it is three apps in one with little for the hands. Award: Best First Five Minutes, weakly.
- **P4 Vacation visualiser.** Flying over photogrammetry is striking for two minutes; then tiles pop in and the motion is uncomfortable. It is another company's product with required attribution. Award: none.
- **P5 Curious Cat.** Touch your cup and a cat explains how ceramics are fired: magical when it works. It needs camera frames and a paid vision model, and three camera-plus-AI apps were already awarded in 2025. In a seat you run out of objects in two minutes. Award: Best Agentic Interaction.
- **P6 Gym.** Nobody wears tethered glasses on a chest press. It needs machine recognition and fails the seated brief entirely. Award: none.
- **P7 Brain exercise.** No mechanic is defined. The calorie premise is not true to a useful degree, and "sharper" claims cost Lumosity an FTC settlement. Award: none.
- **A1 Music visualiser.** Strands of light hang ahead; plucking plays in key, so it sounds good in ten seconds. Conducting needs raised arms. After one piece there is a "what now?". Award: Best First Five Minutes.
- **A2 AI game master.** A diorama on the lap, a narrated scene, three choices, hand-thrown dice. The dice delight; waits and uneven writing are the risk. Award: Best Agentic Interaction.
- **A3 Tabletop duel.** Pull back, release, pucks clack: instantly legible. The link invite needs a second headset owner at the same moment. Award: Best Social & Multiplayer.
- **A4 Ritual object.** A small object on the desk with tasks as tokens. Day one has little payoff, and how tasks get in is unspecified. Award: Best Reason to Come Back.
- **A5 Hand shapes.** Copy a shape and it clicks: the "it sees my fingers" moment is strong. The hand must be raised, tutors exist, and sign content needs Deaf review. Award: Accessibility Forward, arguable.
- **A6 Pop-up book.** A book opens, a tab is pulled, a fox pops up: the most charming first minute here. When the story ends, so does the reason to open it. Award: Best First Five Minutes.
- **A7 Mystery.** Turn a watch over, find an engraving, pin it up. A great verb that needs many bespoke props and good writing. Award: Best Reason to Come Back.
- **A8 Planning board.** Sorting cards by pinch-drag is calm, but getting tasks in needs voice or typing and a phone is faster. Award: none.
- **A9 Comic reader.** Panels unfold in depth and the hands never leave the lap. It depends entirely on a comic the entrant must draw or source. Award: Best First Five Minutes.

## Appendix B — ideas I did not refine, and why

- **G1 Bowling.** The only seated form is a lap-sized pull-and-release, which is A3-R. A separate entry would add nothing.
- **G3 Rafting.** Paddling is repetitive large motion and a moving river causes discomfort; a tabletop toy-boat version removes both and also removes the idea.
- **G4 Runner.** The genre is reaction speed, which hand tracking handles worst. A rhythm version is a different game in a field with Beat Saber Flux at launch.
- **E1 Anime converter.** A live filter cannot be built on this stack. Its salvageable part, authored stylisation of flat art into depth, is in E2-R and A6-R.
- **P2 Gardening.** The honest form is a time-scrub growth model of a few plants. That is a hard procedural-art problem with weak device fit and real accuracy risk.
- **P4 Vacation visualiser.** Its entire value is third-party data, which the rules discourage.
- **A1, A5, A8.** Each is absorbed: A1 into E3-R, A5 into P6-R, A8 into A4-R and the "My Day" deck of P7-R.

## Appendix D — things I could not settle

- **Hover from gaze.** The brief says hover does not fire from gaze. The IWSDK guide says gaze uses the standard `Hovered` and `Pressed` states. Every refined entry works without hover; this needs a device check before any design leans on it.
- **Lap-level tracking on the Glasses.** Whether the Glasses' cameras track hands resting in the lap as well as Quest 3 does is unknown to me. E3-R and P6-R depend on it most.
- **Special award definitions.** Not published where I could find them; my award targets are readings of the names.
- **Field size per track.** My claim that Productivity is the thinnest field is inference from the announced line-up, not from entry counts.
- **Platform reports are undated.** The Web Speech and camera-access findings come from forum threads I could not date; the engineer should confirm both on a current Quest Browser.
